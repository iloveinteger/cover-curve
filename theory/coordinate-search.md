# Coordinate breakpoint search

## 1. Problem

Let
$$
X=(x_0,\ldots,x_n),\qquad a=x_0<x_1<\cdots<x_n=b,
$$
and let $V(X)$ be the exact fixed-breakpoint shared-height optimum.

The free-breakpoint problem is
$$
E_n^*=
\inf_{a<x_1<\cdots<x_{n-1}<b}V(X).
$$

Coordinate search optimizes one interior breakpoint at a time. It is a nonconvex local method.

## 2. Exact coordinate subproblem

Fix all breakpoints except $x_i$. On a compact ordered region with margin $\delta>0$, its admissible interval is
$$
I_i=[x_{i-1}+\delta,\;x_{i+1}-\delta].
$$

Define
$$
\phi_i(t)=
V(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1},t,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n-1}).
$$

An exact coordinate update chooses a global minimizer of $\phi_i$ on $I_i$.

Every trial point supplies all $n+1$ breakpoints to the fixed-breakpoint oracle. Therefore the inner solve has no breakpoint-choice relaxation: the only outer variable changed in this subproblem is $x_i$.

## 3. Descent theorem

### Theorem 1 — monotonicity

Assume the fixed-breakpoint oracle is exact and the coordinate subproblem is solved globally. If $X^{k+1}$ is obtained from $X^k$ by replacing coordinate $x_i^k$ with a global minimizer of $\phi_i$ on its admissible interval, then
$$
V(X^{k+1})\le V(X^k).
$$

### Proof

The old coordinate $x_i^k$ belongs to the same admissible interval. Hence the minimum of $\phi_i$ cannot exceed its value at $x_i^k$:
$$
V(X^{k+1})
=
\min_{t\in I_i}\phi_i(t)
\le
\phi_i(x_i^k)
=
V(X^k).
$$
∎

Since $V(X)\ge0$, the sequence of exact objective values generated by exact coordinate updates is monotone non-increasing and therefore convergent.

This theorem does not imply convergence to $E_n^*$.

## 4. Limit-point theorem

### Theorem 2 — coordinatewise minimality of accumulation points

Assume:

1. all iterates lie in a compact ordered region;
2. $V$ is continuous on that region;
3. each coordinate subproblem is solved globally;
4. the cyclic update sequence has an accumulation point $X^*$.

Then $X^*$ is coordinatewise minimizing: for every interior coordinate $i$ and every admissible $t$,
$$
V(X^*)
\le
V(x_1^*,\ldots,x_{i-1}^*,t,x_{i+1}^*,\ldots,x_{n-1}^*).
$$

### Proof

Consider a subsequence immediately before updates of coordinate $i$ that converges to $X^*$. Global optimality of the coordinate update gives
$$
V(X^k)
\le
V(x_1^k,\ldots,x_{i-1}^k,t,x_{i+1}^k,\ldots,x_{n-1}^k)
$$
for every admissible $t$ and every member of that subsequence.

By continuity of $V$, passage to the limit gives the stated inequality. ∎

If $V$ is differentiable at an interior accumulation point, coordinatewise minimality implies
$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial x_i}(X^*)=0
$$
for every $i$. Thus such a limit point is stationary.

The theorem is local: a coordinatewise minimum need not be a global minimum of a nonconvex function.

## 5. Numerical coordinate minimization

The implementation cannot globally minimize an arbitrary continuous one-dimensional function in finite time. It therefore uses a deterministic coarse-to-fine approximation:

~~~text
for each cyclic sweep:
    for each interior breakpoint i:
        define admissible interval I_i

        sample phi_i on a uniform coarse grid
        choose the best sampled interval
        refine that interval repeatedly
        evaluate the resulting candidate with the fixed-breakpoint oracle

        accept the candidate only if it decreases the incumbent value

    stop when the sweep makes no accepted improvement
~~~

With an exact oracle and refinement mesh tending to zero, continuity of the one-dimensional objective implies convergence of the sampled minimum to the exact coordinate minimum. A finite production budget does not provide a global-optimality certificate.

## 6. Complexity

Let

- $d=n-1$ be the number of interior breakpoints;
- $S$ be the number of coarse samples per coordinate;
- $R$ be the number of refinement rounds;
- $C_{\mathrm{DH}}$ be the cost of one fixed-breakpoint solve.

One full sweep uses
$$
O\bigl(d(S+R)C_{\mathrm{DH}}\bigr)
$$
fixed-breakpoint solves, up to implementation-specific reuse.

For $K$ sweeps,
$$
O\bigl(Kd(S+R)C_{\mathrm{DH}}\bigr).
$$

The method avoids the exponential ambient subdivision tree of direct breakpoint-space exhaustive search, but it sacrifices global optimality.

## 7. Numerical error

For a returned point $X_{\mathrm{out}}$ and numerical value $\widehat V$,
$$
|\widehat V-E_n^*|
\le
|\widehat V-V(X_{\mathrm{out}})|
+
|V(X_{\mathrm{out}})-E_n^*|.
$$

The first term is controlled only by the numerical fixed-breakpoint and coordinate-search tolerances. The second is the nonconvex optimization gap and is not bounded by the coordinatewise stopping test alone.

## 8. Solver relationship

- adaptiveGridDP: breakpoint-grid global method in the exact-oracle model.
- fastGridDP: optimized implementation of the same mathematical target.
- directHeight: fixed-breakpoint continuous-height oracle.
- breakpointSearch: exhaustive direct breakpoint-space cross-check for small $n$.
- coordinateSearch: cyclic one-dimensional nonconvex optimization.
- envelopeSQP: envelope-sensitivity local optimization.

The coordinate solver therefore provides a structurally different local method, not a replacement for the global convergence theorem of the breakpoint-grid method.
